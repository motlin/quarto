How to play

The pieces

Sixteen pieces, each a unique mix of four traits:

A turn

You never place your own piece. On your turn you choose any piece from the tray and hand it to your opponent; they must place it on an empty cell, then choose the next piece for you.

The first player only chooses; the second player makes the first placement.

Winning

Whoever places the fourth piece of a row, column or diagonal in which all four pieces share a trait — all tall, all dark, all round, all hollow, and so on — wins immediately. The board highlights the winning set.

Lines + 2×2 squares is the common advanced variant: any 2×2 block of four cells sharing a trait also wins. It makes the game much sharper; with lines only, perfect play is very drawish.

If the board fills without a win, the game is drawn.

Annotations

The oracle is a solver that searches the whole game tree, so its verdict is exact, not an estimate.

  • Outcome shows who wins with perfect play from both sides and in how many moves — a "move" is one placement. Draw means neither side can force a win.
  • Move values label every legal choice from the point of view of the player to move: W3 wins in 3 moves, L2 loses in 2 against best play, = holds the draw.

Annotations can be switched on and off during a game with the toggle in the top bar. In a two-person game they are off by default so nobody sees the answer unless you both want to.

Playing the engine

The engine plays perfectly from the first move. When the verdict is Draw you are still holding — one slip and it becomes a countdown. Undo takes back your last decision.
